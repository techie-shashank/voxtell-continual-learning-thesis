## 1. Problem and idea (abstract and introduction; p. 1–3)
- Universal segmentation models are normally trained statically on pooled data from all departments, which conflicts with data-sharing rules and with the need to add tasks later (p. 1).
- The authors frame continual training of a universal model as **task-incremental learning (TIL)**: tasks arrive one after another, earlier data are not accessible, and consecutive tasks differ simultaneously in image appearance (modality, region, device) and segmentation objective ("concurrent shifts", p. 2, Fig. 1a p. 1).
- Existing class-incremental methods (distillation, prototypes, prompts) assume little appearance shift; domain-incremental methods (style regularisation, image-only generative replay) assume a fixed objective; neither handles both (p. 2).
- Proposal: **C²GR**, generative replay that synthesises image–mask pairs of earlier tasks with a diffusion model, with two components (p. 2–3): **Bayesian Joint Diffusion (BJD)** to keep image–mask correspondence, and **Relation-aware Unified Prompt Synchronization (RUPS)**, a shared task prompt injected into both generator and segmentor to couple their optimisation.
- Claim of novelty: "to our knowledge the first TIL framework for training a universal segmentation model capable of handling diverse tasks" (p. 2).
- In Sec. 2.3 (p. 3) the authors set themselves apart from SAM-Med3D-MoE, Continual SAM and MedSeqFT, which "only adapt a pre-trained model to incoming tasks on a small number of tasks, rather than developing a universal model across diverse tasks from scratch" (p. 3).
- Journal extension over CGR [19] (p. 3): RUPS (new), 20 tasks over 8 modalities and 9 regions, more baselines and ablations.

## 2. Setting (p. 3, p. 6–7)
- Scenario named by the authors: **task-incremental learning** (p. 2–3, Sec. 3 p. 3). At round t only D^t = {X^t, Y^t} is accessible (p. 3).
- **A task = one dataset or dataset subset** (one site, vendor or MR sequence of a dataset counts as its own task), with its own modality, region and objective; several tasks are multi-class (OC + OD; LV, RV, MYO; kidney + tumour etc.) (Fig. 3 p. 6; Sec. 4.1.1 p. 6).
- **How the target is specified:** per task, a fixed text template "A [I] image of [A] for [SO]" filled with imaging modality, anatomical region and segmentation objective (Sec. 3.2.1 p. 5; Fig. 2b p. 4); for tasks with the same modality from different sites or devices, site/device information is added to the prompt (Sec. 4.2 p. 7). The exact strings per task are not given. The template text is encoded by a **frozen CLIP text encoder**, projected to a graph node, and passed through six attribute subgraphs (modality, region, objective and their pairs) with learned attention, giving a **relation-aware task prompt ζ_i** (Eq. 4–5, p. 5). ζ_i conditions every layer of the segmentor (and of the denoising network) through cross-attention (Eq. 6, p. 6). The prompt is therefore **one per task, built from a template, not a free-text prompt per structure**; the text prompts of all tasks are kept in a "prompt pool" (p. 5).
- **Inference:** not described explicitly. Training and replay use the task prompt of the task concerned (f_θ^{ζ_i}, Eq. 7 p. 6), and the scenario is TIL, so the task (its prompt) is evidently supplied at test time; how a multi-class task's classes are output (channels per task, shared head) is **not stated**.
- **Not few-shot:** official train/test splits, 15 % of the training data for validation; 60 %/15 %/25 % train/val/test where no official split exists (p. 6). The full training sets are used. The replay generates "the same number of image-mask pairs for each past task as in its training set" (p. 7).
- One task order only (Fundus-A → … → ISIC18, Fig. 3 order, p. 7). Order dependence is not studied.

## 3. Method (Sec. 3, p. 3–6; Fig. 2 p. 4)
- **Naive Joint Diffusion (NJD)** (Sec. 3.1.1, p. 4–5): image and mask concatenated along channels and denoised jointly (DDPM-style; Eq. 1). Authors argue that noising both at once destroys the image–mask correspondence.
- **BJD** (Sec. 3.1.2, p. 5): by Bayes, log p(x, y) is decomposed into log p(x)p(y) + log p(x|y) + log p(y|x) (Eq. 2); the loss adds two conditional denoising terms in which the clean mask conditions image denoising and the clean image conditions mask denoising (Eq. 3). Sampling uses the same reverse process as NJD.
- **RUPS** (Sec. 3.2, p. 5–6): relation-aware prompt graph (Eq. 4–5; graph attention mixed with a cosine similarity of attribute components via a learnable weight in [0, 1]; subgraph importance scores aggregated by softmax) produces ζ_i; ζ_i injected by cross-attention into each layer of segmentor and denoiser (Eq. 6).
- **Training round t** (Sec. 3.3, p. 6; Fig. 2c): (1) generate replay sets R^{1…t−1} with the denoiser conditioned on stored earlier prompts; (2) compute the prompt of the current task (the graph spans tasks 1…t, so the prompts of earlier tasks are also recomputed; own reading of Eq. 4–5); (3) jointly optimise segmentor and denoiser on D^t ∪ R: "Memory Evoking" L_ME = L_seg(current) + mean over earlier tasks of L_seg(replay), L_seg = Dice + cross-entropy (Eq. 7); "Memory Updating" L_MU = BJD loss on current + mean over replayed tasks (Eq. 8); total L = L_ME + L_MU (Eq. 9).
- **Trained parameters:** segmentor (all), denoising network (all), prompt graph parameters (projection W_E, subgraph layers V_S, W_q, b_q, q) and the cross-attention Q/K/V; CLIP text encoder frozen (p. 5–6). No pretrained segmentation model; the segmentor is a UNet trained from scratch (p. 3, p. 7). Whether the denoiser is pretrained is not stated. No pseudocode, no complexity analysis.

## 4. Data (Sec. 4.1.1, p. 6–7; Fig. 3 p. 6)
- 20 tasks, 8 modalities (fundus, MR, CT, histopathology, ultrasound, X-ray, endoscopy, dermoscopy), 9 regions (eye, heart, brain, abdomen, mucosa, breast, chest, colon, skin) (Fig. 3; p. 3).
- Sizes from **Fig. 3** (unit, i.e. images vs volumes, and whether the count is the whole dataset or the training set, **not stated**): Fundus-A 101, Fundus-B 159, Fundus-C 400, Fundus-D 400 (optic cup + disc; four sites/cameras of a multi-site fundus dataset [36]); M&Ms-A 95, M&Ms-B 125, M&Ms-C 50, M&Ms-D 50 (cine MR, LV/RV/MYO, vendors Siemens, Philips, GE, Canon); BraTS-T1, -T1ce, -T2, -FLAIR 1,251 each (brain tumour; four MR sequences of BraTS [3], i.e. the same subjects appear in four tasks, own observation); Pancreas 281, KiTS 210, LiTS 131 (CT, organ + tumour); GlaS 165 (histopathology glands); BUSI 647 (ultrasound breast tumour); QaTaV2 9,258 (chest X-ray COVID-19 region); Polyp 1,992 (endoscopy); ISIC18 3,694 (dermoscopy skin lesion).
- **2D only:** each dataset preprocessed with its official pipeline (centre-cropping, resizing, normalisation), and "all images and axial slices of 3D volumes are resized to 256 × 256" (p. 7). The 3D datasets (M&Ms, BraTS, Pancreas, KiTS, LiTS) are therefore treated as stacks of 2D axial slices; the number of slices is not stated. The authors name fixed-resolution preprocessing as a limitation (p. 11).
- Downstream (unseen) tasks, Sec. 4.4.4 (p. 9–10): DRIVE (retinal vessels, fundus), GOALS (retinal layers, OCT), SegPC (cytoplasm and nucleus, microscopy), DDTI (thyroid nodules, ultrasound), PROMISE12 (prostate, MR); sizes not stated.

## 5. Implementation (Sec. 4.2, p. 7)
- Segmentor: UNet [29], channels 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 512, 512. Denoiser: UNet, channels 128, 128, 256, 256, 512, 512. Both with cross-attention of dimension 768.
- Diffusion: K = 1000 forward steps; 200 reverse steps for replay generation.
- Graph node dimension 512; vector q dimension 256.
- Adam, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴, batch size 48, **300 epochs per round**, for both models.
- Hardware: two NVIDIA A100 GPUs. **Training time, generation time and memory: not reported.**
- Seeds / number of runs, variability (± values): **not stated**; all tables report single values.
- How the baselines (incl. MedSeqFT, a foundation-model method, and GR with Stable Diffusion, p. 10) were configured on this backbone: **not stated**.

## 6. Evaluation protocol and metrics (Sec. 4.1.2, p. 7; Eq. 10)
- Segmentation metric: DSC, averaged over foreground classes for multi-class tasks (p. 7). Reported in %.
- Train–test matrix D ∈ ℝ^{T×T}, D_{i,j} = DSC on task j after training up to task i (p. 7).
- **Overall** = mean of the last row, Σ_j D_{T,j} / T (final performance).
- **BWT** = 2 / (T(T−1)) · Σ_{i=2}^{T} Σ_{j=1}^{i−1} (1 − |min(D_{i,j} − D_{j,j}, 0)|) (Eq. 10). Not the Lopez-Paz/Chaudhry BWT: it averages over **all intermediate steps** (every i > j, not only the final row), clips gains to zero, and is expressed as "1 minus drop", so 100 = no forgetting. The "1 −" requires DSC as a fraction, whereas the tables are in % (own observation; the scaling is not explained).
- **TL** ("Transfer Learning") = Σ_i D_{i,i} / T, described as plasticity (p. 7). This is the mean diagonal, i.e. the same quantity as this thesis's Adaptation metric (own observation).
- Reference points: JointTrain (all data pooled, upper reference), FineTune (sequential, lower), IndividualTrain (one model per task, selected at inference) (p. 7).
- **No measure of pretrained or zero-shot ability** (there is no pretrained model). Transfer to unseen tasks is tested only after **fine-tuning** the final model on each downstream task for 60 epochs (lr 10⁻⁴), compared with training from scratch (Sec. 4.4.4, p. 10).
- Generator quality (Sec. 4.4.2, p. 9; Fig. 6b): FID of synthesised vs real images, and DSC between generated masks and JointTrain predictions on the generated images; values only in radar plots.

  - DIL: EWC 26.74 / 36.46 / 81.07; GR 75.54 / 93.78 / 82.52; SR-DSFW 42.71 / 43.66 / 84.37; TED 23.81 / 33.13 / 83.71

  - CGR (conference version) 81.89 / 96.14 / 84.16; **C²GR 83.62 / 98.16 / 85.24**
  - Selected per-task C²GR values: Fundus-A 84.58, BraTS-T1 77.94, Pancreas 73.84, GlaS 84.68, BUSI 71.39, ISIC18 87.53 (FineTune: 27.82, 17.53, 31.73, 47.84, 31.74, 88.02).
- Headline claims checked (own calculations): abstract "only a 2.44% drop … compared to joint training" = 86.06 − 83.62 = 2.44 **percentage points** (relative drop 2.84 %); "surpassing … MedSeqFT by 6.49% in Overall DSC and 2.98% in BWT" = 83.62 − 77.13 and 98.16 − 95.18 (p. 7); "+1.73% Overall" over CGR = 83.62 − 81.89. Overall recomputed as the mean of the 20 per-task C²GR values = 83.62 (consistent).
- Authors' interpretation (p. 7): FineTune collapses on earlier tasks; EWC/TED fail on objective-related knowledge; image-only replay (GR, SR-DSFW) reuses masks biased to the current objective; distillation/prototype CIL methods degrade because of appearance shift; MedSeqFT degrades little but stores raw past data.
- **Ablation** (Fig. 5, p. 8): removing BJD or RUPS lowers performance on earlier tasks; values only as a radar plot. NJD vs BJD (Fig. 6, p. 9): BJD has lower FID and higher mask DSC on all tasks shown (radar plot).
- RUPS analysis (Fig. 7–8, p. 9): plain CLIP prompt embeddings are uniformly similar across tasks; RUPS prompts form blocks by shared region/objective (e.g. the four fundus tasks) and the segmentor features separate tasks better (t-SNE).
- **Table 2 (p. 10), downstream fine-tuning, DSC %** (DRIVE / GOALS / SegPC / DDTI / PROMISE12 / Overall): Scratch 70.73 / 87.81 / 73.21 / 74.49 / 72.08 / 75.66; JointTrain 73.08 / 91.11 / 77.40 / 83.35 / 85.28 / 82.04; FineTune 71.45 / 88.72 / 74.65 / 77.27 / 80.14 / 78.45; GR 72.57 / 90.26 / 75.39 / 78.68 / 81.63 / 79.71; SR-DSFW 65.78 / 86.22 / 76.17 / 82.06 / 84.06 / 78.86; CoNuSeg 72.97 / 90.86 / 76.92 / 77.86 / 81.59 / 80.04; MedSeqFT 71.93 / 89.86 / 75.66 / 79.73 / 84.43 / 80.32; **C²GR 74.29 / 90.76 / 76.33 / 82.25 / 85.68 / 81.86**.

What is counted (segmentor + generator + graph?) is not stated.

### Inconsistencies and weak points (flagged)
- Abstract and p. 7 give differences in "%" that are percentage points.
- "second-best method MedSeqFT" (p. 7) ignores CGR (81.89), the authors' own earlier method, which is second-best in Table 1.
- p. 10 says C²GR "achieves the best performance among all methods" on the downstream tasks; true for Overall among the incremental methods, but CoNuSeg is higher on GOALS (90.86 vs 90.76) and SegPC (76.92 vs 76.33), and JointTrain (82.04) is higher overall (Table 2).
- BWT formula mixes fractions and % (Sec. 6); JointTrain and IndividualTrain get BWT = 100 by construction.
- Claim of being the "first TIL framework for training a universal segmentation model" (p. 2) sits next to Sec. 2.3 (p. 3), which cites three continual-learning works for universal/foundation models; the distinction rests on "from scratch".
- MedSeqFT is described as storing raw past-task data (p. 7); how it, or any baseline, was transferred to the 2D UNet with task prompts is not documented.
- No seeds, no variability, single task order, compute reported only as parameter counts.

## 8. Limitations and future work (authors; Discussion p. 11)
- Fixed-resolution preprocessing (256 × 256) chosen for computational reasons may not match the resolution needs of different modalities; the authors state that this does not change the concurrent shifts (p. 11).
- Future work: variable-resolution inputs under TIL, e.g. transformer backbones with visual token pruning (p. 11).
- Not stated by the authors (own observation): 2D only, no pretrained model, full data, one order, no seeds, replay cost grows with the number of past tasks (the replay set equals the size of all earlier training sets), task identity needed at inference.

## 10. Analysis for this thesis (NOT from the paper; verify before using)
1. **Relation to this thesis.** This thesis studies VoxTell, a pretrained free-text promptable 3D universal segmentation model, adapted sequentially to six tasks (target structures of SKM-TEA knee MRI, one text prompt each) in two task orders (original, reverse), with k-shot support sets, and compares sequential fine-tuning, task-specific LoRA (task-aware inference), shared LoRA, shared LoRA + replay, the distillation-based strategy (adapted from ZSCL) and the prompt-tuning strategy (adapted from CPE-CLIP), measuring Adaptation, Final Forgetting and the drop on a held-out control task (AeroPath, "lung"). C²GR shares only "continual learning of a text-conditioned universal segmentation network"; it differs on every other axis:
   - (a) **Target specification:** one fixed template prompt per task ("A [I] image of [A] for [SO]", plus site/device), CLIP-encoded and turned into a learned task prompt; supplied at inference as task identity. Not free text, not per structure (multi-class tasks share one prompt).
   - (b) **Data:** 2D, 256 × 256; 3D datasets reduced to axial slices; 20 tasks across 8 modalities; sizes per Fig. 3 (unit unstated).
   - (c) **Model:** UNet segmentor trained from scratch, plus a UNet denoiser, both fully trained; no pretrained universal model, so no pretrained ability to protect.
   - (d) **Scenario:** TIL; a task is a dataset or subset (site, vendor, sequence); task prompt known at test time. Closest to this thesis's task-aware inference, but with shared parameters.
   - (e) **Full data**, no few-shot; the generator even replays as many pairs per past task as its full training set.
   - (f) Forgetting via a clipped all-steps BWT (100 = none); final performance (Overall) and mean diagonal (TL, = this thesis's Adaptation). No control task, no zero-shot measure; transfer only after fine-tuning on unseen tasks.
   - (g) Baselines: JointTrain, FineTune, IndividualTrain, EWC, GR, SR-DSFW, TED, PLOP, MEIL, CoNuSeg, MedSeqFT, CGR; C²GR 83.62 % Overall vs JointTrain 86.06 %, MedSeqFT 77.13 %, FineTune 25.23 %.
   - (h) **Generator:** UNet denoiser replays synthetic image–mask pairs for all earlier tasks, conditioned on their task prompts, 200 reverse steps, as many pairs as each task's training set; generator trained jointly at every round; cost reported only as ≈ 400 M learnable parameters (Fig. 9), no time or memory.
2. **Verdict on the gap claim.** C²GR does **not** falsify or materially weaken the claim at `related_work.tex:117`; it is further from the thesis than MedCRP-CL (`paper_notes/NEW_medcrpcl2026.md`), which at least continually adapts a **pretrained** text-promptable model (2D CLIPSeg) with sentence-like prompts. C²GR has neither a pretrained model nor free text nor 3D. Its risk is rhetorical: its title and its claim to be the "first TIL framework for training a universal segmentation model" (p. 2) use "universal", so the thesis should keep the distinction between **training a universal model from scratch by continual learning** (C²GR) and **continually adapting a pretrained free-text promptable universal model** (this thesis). Keeping "pretrained" (or "adaptation of a … model") and "free-text" in the gap sentence covers it; the reworded gap suggested in the MedCRP-CL notes (§10.3) also covers C²GR.
3. **Comparison with the two other near-misses.**
   - *MedCRP-CL* (2D, pretrained CLIPSeg frozen, modality-specific LoRA, clinical text prompts, full data, no pretrained-ability measure): closer on "pretrained promptable model"; C²GR closer on "shared parameters with task prompt" and has many more baselines and tasks. Neither is 3D, few-shot, or measures loss of pretrained ability.
   - *CLIP-Driven Universal Model, MedIA 2024* (`paper_notes/cards/NEW_liu2024universal.md`): 3D CT, class-incremental, fixed CLIP template embedding per **class**, new MLP/head per class, pseudo-label supervision; C²GR's template idea explicitly follows this line (it cites [21], the ICCV 2023 version, for describing a task by modality, region and objective, p. 5). Liu et al. is 3D but closed-set and class-wise; C²GR is 2D and task-wise. Neither uses free text or a pretrained model to be preserved, neither is few-shot.
   - Together the three show that "text-conditioned segmentation + continual learning" exists (2D pretrained, 2D from scratch, 3D class-template); what none covers is the combination pretrained free-text promptable **3D** model + few-shot tasks + measurement of retained pretrained ability (control task).
4. **Useful for design decisions / discussion:**
   - TL (mean diagonal) is a published precedent for the thesis's Adaptation metric; Overall corresponds to Final Performance. Their BWT variant (all steps, clipped) differs from the Chaudhry-style Final Forgetting used here; if cited, say so.
   - JointTrain/IndividualTrain as reference points parallel the joint-training reference discussed for this thesis.
   - Evidence that image-only or regularisation methods degrade badly when tasks differ in both appearance and objective (Table 1), in 2D with full data; in this thesis all tasks share one MRI dataset, so appearance shift is small and only the objective changes. Do not transfer the numbers.
   - Generative replay as an alternative to stored-volume replay (shared LoRA + replay): C²GR shows it can work in 2D but needs a second large network trained every round and full-size replay sets; a plausible argument for why generative replay was not considered for few-shot 3D tasks (author's decision; few-shot data would hardly suffice to train a 3D generator, which is an assumption, not tested).
   - Single order and no seeds: a concrete contrast to this thesis's two task orders.
5. **Bib:** no entry; venue not identified (journal extension of MICCAI 2024 CGR). If cited, check whether a journal version has appeared; otherwise cite arXiv:2606.23473v1 and, for the mechanism, possibly the peer-reviewed MICCAI 2024 CGR paper (pp. 80–90), whose content (no RUPS, fewer tasks) must then be checked separately.
