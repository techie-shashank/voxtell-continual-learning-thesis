## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: class-incremental semantic segmentation of abdominal organs, where earlier data are inaccessible for privacy reasons and labels of earlier classes are absent in later steps (Abstract; Sec. 1, p. 51; Sec. 2.1, p. 52). The authors claim to be the first to use generative replay for incremental class learning in semantic segmentation (Abstract; Sec. 1).
- Data (Sec. 3, p. 54): BTCV, 30 abdominal CT scans from Vanderbilt University Medical Center, 13 organs; first 25 scans for training, last 5 as validation set; the 3D images are converted to 2D slices and slices without organs are removed. Scenarios: 13-1, 9-5, 8-2 (classes added per step) (Sec. 3.2, p. 54).
- Method (two sentences): at each step a GAN ("SegGAN", modified from Mescheder et al.) is trained on 4-channel inputs that stack the image and its label, so that it generates image–label pairs; the generator of the previous step synthesises old data for replay, and also augments the current step's data (Sec. 2.2–2.2.3, pp. 52–53; Fig. 1). A filtering mechanism keeps a generated pair only if the label generated by SegGAN and the pseudo-label predicted by the segmentation model on the generated image are consistent (difference below threshold 0.5; the exact distance measure is not legible in the extracted Algorithm 1), and training uses MiB's background-aware classification and distillation losses plus a Dice loss (Sec. 2.2.2, 2.2.4, Algorithm 1, Eq. 2–6, pp. 52–54).
- Setting: 2D DeepLab-v3 with ResNet-101; SGD, lr 0.1 first step, 0.01 later; 120 / 60 epochs; batch 8; 100 replayed generated pairs; 150 / 50 augmentation samples; SegGAN 2000 epochs, RMSprop, lr 1e-4 (Sec. 3.1, p. 54). Not 3D, not few-shot, not text-promptable. Number of runs / variability: not stated.
- Evaluation: mean Dice on the validation set, for old classes, new classes and all (Sec. 3.1; Table 1, p. 55). Baselines: fine-tuning (FT), LwF, LwF-MC, ILT, MiB, MiB+Dice loss, Joint, Joint+DL (Sec. 3.2).
- Best result (Table 1, p. 55; PDF checked): mean Dice over all classes, ours vs MiB+DL vs MiB vs Joint+DL: 13-1 0.6139 / 0.5878 / 0.5233 / 0.7219; 9-5 0.6421 / 0.5435 / 0.4117 / 0.7219; 8-2 0.4930 / 0.3840 / 0.2420 / 0.7219. FT 0.0104 / 0.0673 / 0.0030. Stated relative improvement over MiB+DL "about" 4.4 % (13-1), 18.1 % (9-5), 28.4 % (8-2), growing with more steps (p. 55). Ablation (Table 2): generative replay alone helps in all scenarios, filtering further improves the average, augmentation helps most in 9-5 and 8-2.
- Limitations stated by the authors: none explicit; future work is extension to other modalities and tasks (Sec. 4, p. 55). The method stays well below joint training in 8-2 (0.4930 vs 0.7219).

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
The example of replay without stored patient data; in this thesis's setting (3D, few-shot, a large pretrained universal model) training a generator per task is not realistic with a handful of volumes, which is one argument for real-sample replay; that argument would be the author's, not the paper's.
