## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: continual fine-tuning of CLIP for image classification such that both downstream-task knowledge and pre-training (zero-shot) knowledge are kept, without access to the pre-training data and without storing real samples (Abstract; Sec. 1).
- Data (Sec. 4.1; Suppl. B, C): MTIL benchmark taken from ZSCL, 11 datasets with 1,201 classes in total (Aircraft, Caltech101, CIFAR100, DTD, EuroSAT, Flowers, Food, MNIST, OxfordPet, StanfordCars, SUN397), one dataset per task. Two task orders: Order I alphabetical, Order II random (StanfordCars, Food, MNIST, OxfordPet, Flowers, SUN397, Aircraft, Caltech101, DTD, EuroSAT, CIFAR100) (Suppl. C). CIL benchmarks in the supplement: CIFAR100 split into 10/20/50 steps, TinyImageNet with 100 base classes and 5/10/20 steps (Suppl. B, Tables 7–8).
- Method (two sentences): before each task, Stable Diffusion v1.5 generates 1K images from class names sampled from a pool of ImageNet class names plus the class names of all earlier tasks; the images are discarded after the task, so only class names are kept (Sec. 3.2, "Image Generation"; Sec. 4.1). All CLIP parameters (ViT-B/16) are fine-tuned with cross-entropy plus a contrastive distillation loss (KL between the batch image–text similarity matrices of the current model and the model after the previous task, Eq. 2–4), an image–text alignment loss towards the identity matrix (Eq. 5), and an l2 penalty to the previous weights weighted by a Fisher information recomputed during training from the distillation loss on the synthetic pairs ("adaptive weight consolidation", Eq. 8–9) (Sec. 3.1–3.3; Fig. 2).
- Setting: Task-Incremental Learning (MTIL, task identity given, prediction within the task's classes) and Class-Incremental Learning (no task identity) as defined in Sec. 3.1; no few-shot setting. Training: 1K iterations, batch size 64 per MTIL task; α = 1, β = 0.25; AdamW, learning rate searched in {1e-5, 1e-6, 1e-7}, label smoothing 0.2 (Sec. 4.1; Suppl. A).
- Evaluation (Sec. 4.1; Suppl. C, Eq. 10–12, on accuracy matrix a_{i,j} = accuracy on task j after learning task i): "Transfer" = mean of the upper triangle (accuracy on tasks not yet learned, zero-shot ability); "Last" = mean accuracy on all tasks after the final task; "Avg." = mean over the whole matrix. In CIL: "Avg." = mean accuracy over all steps, "Last" = after the final step. Baselines on MTIL: zero-shot CLIP, continual fine-tune, l2 baseline, LwF, iCaRL, LwF-VR, WiSE-FT, ZSCL, MoE-Adapter (Tables 1–2).
- Best result: MTIL Order I Transfer 69.3, Avg. 77.3, Last 86.0 (%) vs ZSCL 68.1/75.4/83.6 and MoE-Adapter 68.9/76.7/85.0; zero-shot CLIP Transfer 69.4 (Table 1). Order II: 65.9/75.7/85.3 vs ZSCL 64.2/74.5/83.4, zero-shot Transfer 65.4 (Table 2). With 1K synthetic images GIFT outperforms ZSCL with 100K ImageNet images (Sec. 4.2; Table 5). CIFAR100 CIL, 10 steps: Avg. 85.11, Last 77.70 vs ZSCL 82.15/73.65 (Table 7, PDF p. 12, checked on the page image). TinyImageNet 5 steps: 81.16/77.04 vs ZSCL 80.27/73.57 (Table 8).
- Limitations stated by the authors: no limitations section. In Suppl. E (Fig. 9) they note many unsatisfactory synthetic images for DTD and EuroSAT, that Stable Diffusion cannot produce accurate digits (unsuitable for MNIST), and that it does not reproduce the low resolution of CIFAR-100 images, though the classes are captured. Gains from more synthetic images plateau at about 1K per task (Sec. 4.4; Fig. 3). Synthetic images carry more noise than real ImageNet images (Sec. 4.4, Table 5).

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
A full-fine-tuning, distillation-based method like ZSCL that replaces the reference set with generated images; for 3D medical segmentation a comparable text-to-volume generator is not available, which limits direct transfer to this thesis's setting.
