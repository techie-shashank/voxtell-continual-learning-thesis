## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: replay-based continual learning for 3D binary medical segmentation, where randomly stored volumes tend to under-represent small targets and full volumes waste memory (Sec. 1, pp. 514–515; Sec. 2, p. 515).
- Data (Sec. 3.1, pp. 517–518): seven public datasets. Three experiments: (i) domain-incremental prostate, Prostate158 → NCI-ISBI → Promise12 → Decathlon; (ii) domain-incremental hippocampus, HarP → Drayd; (iii) "task incremental" segmentation of three organs, Promise12 (prostate) → MSD (spleen) → Drayd (hippocampus). Numbers of volumes and splits: not stated in the main text.
- Method (two sentences): every training volume gets two scores, Positive Class Ranking (fraction of foreground voxels in the label, Eq. 1) and Gradient Based Ranking (absolute change of the per-sample gradient accumulated over epochs, Algorithm 1, as a proxy for difficulty); half of the p memory slots are filled by each ranking (GPCS/GPCC, Sec. 2.1–2.3, pp. 515–517). Instead of full volumes, crops of h_c × w_c × D around the region of interest are stored, with a foreground–background ratio fbr that adds background crops (Eq. 2, p. 517).
- Setting: Residual UNet; Adam, lr 0.001, weight decay 1e-5, DiceCE loss; crops 160 × 160 (prostate, task-incremental) or 128 × 128 (hippocampus); fbr 0.7 in the task-incremental experiment, 1.0 otherwise (Sec. 3.3, p. 518). Old data are otherwise inaccessible (Sec. 2, p. 515). Not few-shot, not text-promptable. All values are means over four random seeds (footnote 1, p. 518).
- Evaluation: DSC per dataset plus ACC (average accuracy), BWT and AFGT (average forgetting) (Sec. 3.2, p. 518). Baselines: Individual, Joint, Sequential (SGD), L2 regularisation, EWC, Representation Replay, Random Replay(3), where 3 is the number of full volumes stored (Tables 2–4, footnote 2, p. 519).
- Best result: task-incremental (Table 4, p. 521): GPCC Replay(12) ACC 83.3 ± 1.3, BWT −4.2, AFGT 4.2 vs Random Replay(3) 78.3 ± 1.3, AFGT 13.1; Sequential 37.9 (prostate and spleen DSC 0.0); EWC 42.6; L2 49.4; GPCC is up to 32 % lighter in memory (p. 521). Prostate domain-incremental (Table 2, p. 519): GPCC(6) ACC 82.8 ± 1.3 vs Random Replay(3) 78.4 ± 2.8 and Sequential 73.6 ± 2.3; six 160 × 160 × D crops are 65 % lighter than three full volumes (p. 519). Hippocampus (Table 3, p. 520): GPCC(6) ACC 79.8 ± 0.6 vs Random Replay(3) 78.7 ± 1.4, 26 % less memory. Ablation (Table 1, p. 519): PCR and GBR alone improve ACC over random replay by 2.8 and 3.2 points; GPCC by 4.4.
- Limitations stated by the authors: none stated (Sec. 5, p. 521 is a summary). Observed in the tables: the memory sizes differ between GPCC (6 or 12 crops) and Random Replay (3 volumes), so the comparison is at different item counts, matched only roughly by memory; the joint-training rows are not consistently above GPCC (e.g. Table 4 Joint spleen 80.5 vs GPCC 87.9), and the paper does not explain this.

## Check of the author's notes
- related_work.tex:79 "Replay reintroduces data from earlier tasks and is constrained in volumetric imaging by memory as well as by privacy, since a single scan contains a large number of voxels while the target may occupy a small fraction of it" → correct as motivation of Bera et al.: privacy and unavailability of old data (Sec. 1, p. 514); small target, e.g. hippocampus under 0.5 % of the area, memory wastage (Sec. 2.3, p. 517).
- related_work.tex:79 "Bera et al. therefore stored crops around the relevant anatomical region rather than complete scans" → correct (Sec. 2.3, p. 517); in the task-incremental experiment some background crops are also stored (fbr 0.7, Sec. 3.3).
- related_work.tex:79 "selecting them by training difficulty and target presence" → partly correct: difficulty is right (gradient-based ranking); the second criterion is the fraction of foreground voxels (positive-class occupancy, Eq. 1, p. 516), not mere presence. Suggest "the proportion of target voxels".
- related_work.tex:79 "and reported an average accuracy of 83.3±1.3 % against 78.3±1.3 % for random replay on a three-task organ-incremental benchmark" → correct numbers (Table 4, p. 521). Two precisions: ACC is the average DSC over tasks; the paper calls the setting "task incremental"; and random replay stores 3 full volumes while GPCC stores 12 crops (footnote 2, p. 519), which the sentence could mention because the comparison is not at equal item count.
- related_work.tex:194 (table) "Three-task organ-incremental benchmark | Replay of selected crops | 83.3 % (random 78.3 %)" → correct (Table 4); the paper also has two domain-incremental benchmarks (prostate, hippocampus) that the row omits. "Three-task" could name the datasets (Promise12 → MSD spleen → Drayd).
- introduction.tex: not cited (grep, 2026-10-23).

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
Direct prior work for the replay strategy: it shows that what is stored in a small 3D memory matters, which is relevant to the choice and size of the replay memory in shared LoRA with replay; it uses a UNet trained from scratch, with no pretrained or text-promptable model and no few-shot data.
