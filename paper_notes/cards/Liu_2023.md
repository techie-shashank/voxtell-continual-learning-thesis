## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: one model for organ segmentation and tumour detection in abdominal CT trained on many partially labelled public datasets; one-hot labels ignore semantic relations between classes and partial labels conflict (Abstract; Sec. 1).
- Data (Abstract; Fig. 2; Sec. 4): training on 14 public CT datasets, 3,410 scans (e.g. 1000 AbdomenCT-1K, 947 MSD, 500 AMOS, 300 KiTS, 201 LiTS, 150 WORD, 140 CT-ORG, 82 Pancreas-CT, 50 BTCV, 40 CHAOS; Fig. 2), 25 organs and 6 tumour types, 3D. Testing on 6,162 external CT scans: 3D-IRCADb (100), TotalSegmentator (1024), JHH (5038, private) (Fig. 2). Five-fold cross-validation (Sec. 4).
- Method (two sentences): a text branch takes the frozen CLIP text embedding of a prompt such as "A computerized tomography of a [CLS]", concatenates it with the globally pooled image feature and maps it via an MLP ("text-based controller") to the parameters of three 1×1×1 convolutions that produce a sigmoid mask per class (Sec. 3.2). Partial labels are handled by masked back-propagation (BCE only on classes annotated in that dataset) (Sec. 3.2).
- Setting: text conditioning with a fixed vocabulary of class names inserted into a prompt template (Table 1; Sec. 3.2); no continual learning; no few-shot. Backbones: Swin UNETR and U-Net (Fig. 8).
- Evaluation: DSC, NSD (segmentation); sensitivity, specificity, harmonic mean (tumour detection) (Sec. 4). Baselines: nnU-Net, Swin UNETR, UNETR, nnFormer, DiNTS, TransBTS, etc. (Tables 2–5); for transfer: scratch, MedicalNet, Models Genesis, Swin UNETR, UniMiSS (Table 6).
- Best result: ranked first on MSD leaderboard and BTCV (Sec. 4.1); BTCV 5-fold average DSC 86.13 vs 82.06 Swin UNETR (Table 3); MSD validation, liver/pancreas/colon tumours +14/+8/+11 % DSC over Swin UNETR (Fig. 3); external 3D-IRCADb mDSC* 91.62 and JHH mDSC 84.54, best among compared models (Table 5); fine-tuning to TotalSegmentator subsets 86.49 / 89.57 / 94.43 / 88.95 DSC (Table 6); label-encoding ablation one-hot 70.42 vs CLIP V3 76.11 DSC (Table 1); more than 6× faster than dataset-specific models by FLOPs (Fig. 7).
- Limitations stated by the authors: prompt template choice matters (Sec. 3.2, Table 1); quality of pseudo-labels for 19 organs and 6 tumours not compared with humans (footnote 7, p. 5). No limitations section.

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
First 3D CT model where a text embedding selects the target class, but with a closed vocabulary and template prompts; it is the conceptual step between one-hot multi-dataset models and free-text promptable models such as VoxTell.
