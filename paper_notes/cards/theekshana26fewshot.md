## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: a method-oriented review of few-shot medical image segmentation (FSS), plus re-runs of a few models (Abstract; Sec. 1, pp. 11:2–11:3). The period is given as 2020 to early 2025 (Abstract) and as 2019 to early 2025 (Sec. 1, p. 11:2; Sec. 5, p. 11:25); "over 90" (Sec. 3, p. 11:10) or "nearly 90" articles (Sec. 5, p. 11:25). The authors claim to be the first method-oriented review of FSS (Sec. 1, p. 11:2).
- Definition of "shot" (Sec. 1, p. 11:2; Sec. 2.2, p. 11:5; Sec. 2.2.1, p. 11:6): an N-way K-shot task, N the number of classes in the support set, K the number of examples per class, K small. FSS in this sense means that at test time the model meets novel classes never seen in training (C_tr ∩ C_te = ∅), usually with episodic training on support and query sets; the test uses the same episodic procedure on unseen classes. Adaptation to the novel class may update weights by gradient methods or use a non-gradient mechanism (Sec. 2.2, p. 11:5). Zero-shot: no prior examples, completely unseen classes (footnote 3, p. 11:24).
- Taxonomy (Sec. 3, pp. 11:8–11:10; Fig. 5, Fig. 6): two cross-cutting views. (a) By methodological intervention (Fig. 6): architectural modifications (Sec. 3.1), subdivided into learnable modules [M], multi-view generation [V] and prior generation [P] (p. 11:11; Tables 2–3); loss-based approaches and learning strategies (Sec. 3.2), subdivided into direct loss functions (contrastive, regularisation; Table 4, p. 11:15) and self-supervised learning (superpixel/supervoxel pretext tasks, Sec. 3.2.2); and meta-learning frameworks (Sec. 3.3). (b) By meta-learning framework (Sec. 3.3, Table 5, p. 11:19): prototypical learning (most common), MAML-type gradient-based methods, foundation models (SAM/MedSAM adaptation, SAM combined with CLIP-type language models, e.g. SaLIP, MedCLIP-SAM), and "transfer learning and other" (supervised U-Net/ResNet models without a meta-learning algorithm). The text calls these "three main categories" but lists four (p. 11:8). Earlier general-FSL categories quoted from Wang et al.: data manipulation, model/architectural, algorithmic (Sec. 2.2, p. 11:5).
- What it says about 3D / volumetric FSS: CT/MRI are volumetric, but 2D slices are often extracted, which ignores inter-slice correlations (Sec. 1, p. 11:2); inter-slice correlations and 3D continuity are "overlooked" in current research (key takeaways, p. 11:3; Sec. 5.2, p. 11:26). Examples: MA-SAM adds 3D convolution adapters to frozen SAM blocks for 3D segmentation (Sec. 3.1.1, p. 11:11); VISA-FSS adds an L2.5D loss on predictions of 2m+1 adjacent slices (m typically 3) as a model-agnostic refinement for volumetric data (Sec. 3.2.2, Eq. 6, p. 11:18); supervoxel-based SSL (Sec. 3.2.2); MetaMedSeg for volumetric organ segmentation (Sec. 3.3.2, p. 11:20); Kim et al. use prototypes per 2D slice plus GRUs across adjacent slices (Sec. 5.2, p. 11:26). Future directions: treat CT/MR as 3D problems and design 3D SSL pretext tasks (Sec. 5.2).
- Other statements relevant here: MAML-type methods may be of limited use in clinical settings because they need post-adaptation (Sec. 3.3.2, p. 11:20); SAM-based methods mostly show zero-shot ability without specific modifications for one-shot or few-shot settings, which the authors call a major limitation (Sec. 5.4, p. 11:27); rapid adaptation of foundation models that preserves their zero-shot ability is underexplored (Sec. 5.6, p. 11:28). Most studies train on healthy-subject data and focus on CT/MRI organs (Sec. 5.1, pp. 11:25–11:26).
- Comparative study (Sec. 4, pp. 11:21–11:25; Table 6, p. 11:23): one-way one-shot, 100 epochs. (i) Abdominal CT (MICCAI-2015 multi-atlas, 13 classes), left and right kidney held out as unseen classes, following SSL-ALP-Net: Dice ALP / SSL-ALP / RecM / PFE = LK 30.91 / 63.34 / 62.11 / 0.00; RK 27.21 / 52.11 / 47.33 / 1.34; spleen 40.31 / 60.99 / 55.24 / 1.22; liver 55.01 / 74.21 / 78.91 / 0.76. SSL with superpixels gives almost 30 Dice points on the kidneys and about 20 on spleen and liver; the natural-image model PFE-Net fails. (ii) Kidney tumour, one-shot: ALP-Net 30.45 vs SSL-ALP-Net 23.94 Dice; superpixel SSL struggles with small, irregular tumours (Sec. 4.2, p. 11:23). The dataset is called KiTS-2019 in Sec. 4 (p. 11:21) and KiTS-2023 in Sec. 4.2 and 5.1. (iii) Qualitative tests of SAM and SegGPT demos only (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 12). Number of runs / variability: not stated.
- Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. 5, pp. 11:25–11:29): few modalities beyond CT/MRI; mostly healthy subjects; 3D structure ignored; even FSS models need sizeable training data for their trainable parts (Sec. 5.3); limited code availability hinders reproduction (Sec. 5.7).

## Check of the author's notes
- introduction.tex:24 "Few-shot learning refers to the adaptation of a model using only a small number of labelled examples from the target task" → partly correct: the review defines few-shot as N-way K-shot with K small examples per class (Sec. 2.2.1, p. 11:6), but in its sense the target classes are novel and unseen during training, typically with episodic support/query training, and the "adaptation" need not update weights (prototypical methods do not fine-tune, Sec. 3, p. 11:10). The thesis's setting (fine-tuning a pretrained model on k labelled volumes per task) is closer to what the review calls transfer learning / "Other" (Sec. 3.3.4) or gradient-based adaptation. The sentence should say that the thesis uses the term in this broader sense and state the number of shots.
- related_work.tex: not cited (grep, 2026-10-23); no summary-table row.

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
The review is the source for the standard N-way K-shot vocabulary and for the claim that 3D few-shot segmentation is under-studied; its own FSS definition (novel classes, episodic, often training-free at test time) differs from the thesis's fine-tuning protocol, which an examiner may question, so the thesis should make its own definition of "shot" (labelled volumes per task) explicit and contrast it with the episodic definition.
