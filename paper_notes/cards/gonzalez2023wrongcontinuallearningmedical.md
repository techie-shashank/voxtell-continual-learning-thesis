## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: the authors argue that continual-learning methods for segmentation focus on preventing forgetting rather than achieving positive backward transfer, and that they should be compared against a multi-model benchmark (one model state per training stage) instead of only against joint training; they propose UNEG and an evaluation flowchart (Abstract; Sec. 1; Sec. 2.3–2.4; Fig. 3).
- Data (Sec. 5.1, p. 7): 3D MRI, incremental domain scenario (same label, different acquisition sources). Prostate: T2-weighted MRI from five sites, 12 to 30 cases each, order BIDMC → I2CVB → HK → UCL → RUNMC; labels central gland and peripheral zone. Hippocampus: DecathHip → Dryad → HarP (case numbers not stated); labels anterior and posterior hippocampus. Right ventricle: M&Ms challenge, two datasets of 75 samples each (Siemens, Philips). Train/test split not stated. One task order per anatomy; order dependence not studied.
- Method (two sentences): a 3D full-resolution nnU-Net is trained sequentially over the domains (250 epochs each) and its state is saved after each stage, so model F_i is initialised from F_{i−1} and fine-tuned on domain i (Sec. 2.3, p. 4; Sec. 5.2). For each stage an autoencoder with the same nnU-Net architecture (last layer changed, MSE loss) is trained, and at test time the segmenter whose autoencoder gives the lowest reconstruction error is used (Sec. 4.1, Eq. 2; Sec. 5.4).
- Setting: no text conditioning; scenario "incremental domain" in the paper's terms (van de Ven taxonomy), split into "Domain Knowledge" (identity given) and "No Domain Knowledge" (identity inferred by the oracle) (Sec. 2.1; Fig. 4); UNEG infers domain identity at inference; no few-shot setting.
- Evaluation: Dice of the final model state on all domains (Figs. 6–8); BWT as in González et al. (Lifelong nnU-Net) and oracle accuracy (Table 1). Baselines: sequential fine-tuning, EWC (λ = 0.4), LwF (T = 2), MiB (α = 0.9, KD weight 1), joint training as upper bound, all from Lifelong nnU-Net defaults (Sec. 5.3); oracle variants AlexNet z-CNN, MONAI autoencoder, CNN, nnU-Net z-CNN (Sec. 5.4).
- Best result (Table 1, p. 9; mean Dice ± std over the domains of the final states): UNEG prostate 87.0 ± 6.5 (oracle accuracy 68.4 %, BWT −3.8 ± 5.9 %), hippocampus 89.0 ± 3.1 (97.4 %, BWT −2.6 ± 2.6 %), right ventricle 88.7 ± 2.3 (90.0 %, BWT −2.0 ± 0.0 %); with true task identity 89.6 ± 2.0, 90.6 ± 2.2, 89.6 ± 1.5. On prostate the AlexNet z-CNN oracle has higher Dice (87.2) and accuracy (84.2 %). Against EWC, LwF and MiB the comparison is given only as box plots (Figs. 6–8): UNEG "considerably better" on prostate, also better on hippocampus; for right ventricle all methods perform similarly, only sequential and LwF visibly lose (Sec. 6.1). No numeric Dice for the CL baselines.
- Limitations stated by the authors: one extra autoencoder must be trained per stage, adding compute (Sec. 6.4); storage grows linearly with the number of domains, and no positive backward transfer is possible for the multi-model benchmark (Sec. 4, p. 6); gap to joint training remains on prostate (Sec. 6.1).

## Check of the author's notes
- related_work.tex:83 "The earliest systems assigned each task a complete model of its own." → not supported by this paper as a chronological claim; the paper presents the multi-model benchmark as a baseline that existing work rarely compares against (Sec. 2.3), not as an early system. Also, the stored models are not independent: each F_i is fine-tuned from F_{i−1} (Sec. 2.3, p. 4).
- related_work.tex:83 "González et al.\ maintained task-specific U-Net experts" → partly correct: one nnU-Net state per training stage, but the stages are domains (acquisition sites) of the same structure, not tasks in this thesis's sense (different structures). The paper says "domain"; "task-specific" should be "domain-specific" or "stage-specific".
- related_work.tex:83 "and used an auxiliary reconstruction network to infer the domain of an incoming image and route it to the corresponding expert" → correct (Sec. 4.1, Eq. 2); the autoencoder is one per domain and replicates the nnU-Net architecture.
- related_work.tex:83 "evaluated on hippocampus, prostate, and cardiac segmentation" → correct (Sec. 5.1); cardiac means right ventricle, all MRI.
- related_work.tex:83 "Retention in these systems is close to complete, since earlier components are never updated" → partly correct for UNEG: the stored states are not updated, but routing errors cost retention (prostate oracle accuracy 68.4 %, BWT −3.8 ± 5.9 %, Dice 87.0 vs 89.6 with true identity; Table 1).
- related_work.tex:83 "the cost is that model size grows with the number of tasks and that nothing learned for one task benefits the others" → correct for UNEG (Sec. 4, p. 6: storage grows linearly; no positive backward transfer). Forward transfer does exist, since F_i starts from F_{i−1}.

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
Supports the argument that a strategy should be compared against storing one parameter set per task (here: task-specific LoRA with task-aware inference); its setting is domain-incremental with one structure, unlike the structure-wise tasks in this thesis.
