## Facts (from the paper, each with location: Sec./p./Table/Fig.)
- Task / problem: continual visual instruction tuning (CVIT) of a multimodal large language model, i.e. learning a sequence of vision–language instruction tasks without forgetting earlier ones, while keeping the parameter growth of architecture expansion small (Abstract; Introduction, p. 1).
- Data ("Datasets", p. 4): CVIT Benchmark (from SMoLoRA, Wang et al. 2024d), six instruction datasets trained in the order ScienceQA, TextVQA, Flickr30k, ImageNet, GQA, VQAv2 (column order of Table 1; "after training on the last task, VQAv2", p. 5). Two settings: Single-type instruction and Five-type instruction (p. 5). Dataset sizes and whether other task orders were tested: not stated.
- Method (two sentences): the base model (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, first stage, without instruction tuning) is frozen, and LoRA-style adapters are inserted into the FFN layers of the LLM and the vision–LLM projection layer; the LoRA matrix A is shared across all tasks (motivated by high cross-task CKA similarity of A in per-task LoRA, Fig. 1), and the matrix B of task i is the shared basis B0 plus a task-specific low-rank product B̃_iÃ_i, mixed by a learnable per-layer coefficient α, ΔW_i = (αB0 + (1−α)B̃_iÃ_i)A, α initialised as Sigmoid(N(0,1)) (Eq. 3–6, p. 3, checked on the page image; Implementation Details, p. 5). A cosine-regularised basis stability loss (1 − sim_t)·‖B0^t − B0^{t−1}‖², with sim_t the cosine similarity between the current and previous task-specific products, limits drift of B0 (Eq. 7–8; Alg. 1). Ranks r = 128, r̃ = r/2; Adam, lr 2e-5, batch 64, one epoch per task (p. 5).
- Setting: CVIT with dynamic architecture expansion (task-specific modules added per task) (Introduction; Methodology). How the task-specific matrices are selected at inference (task identity given or inferred): not stated. The paper states the adapters can be merged into the pretrained weights at inference (p. 6). No few-shot setting.
- Evaluation ("AP and MAP", "BWT", "MIF", p. 4): AP_k = mean accuracy over tasks 1..k after task k; MAP_k = mean of AP_1..AP_k; BWT = mean of (a_{k,j} − a_{j,j}) over earlier tasks (Eq. 10); MIF = mean fraction of outputs that satisfy each task's instruction format (Eq. 11). Per-task metric for Flickr30k not stated in the text; its values exceed 100 (Table 1), so it is not a percentage accuracy. Baselines: Zero-shot (lower reference), DirLoRA (one independent LoRA per task, upper reference), SeqLoRA (one shared LoRA trained sequentially), DoRA, C-LoRA, EWC, Replay, MoeLoRA, EWC+TIR, Eproj, SMoLoRA (p. 4).
- Best result: Single-type: LiLoRA AP 84.88, MAP 87.70, BWT −3.13, MIF 98.24 vs SMoLoRA 83.44 / 84.85 / −3.23 / 97.79, SeqLoRA 46.21 / 57.41 / −48.10 / 78.35, DirLoRA (upper reference) 88.13 / 90.18 / 0.00 / 98.41 (Table 1, p. 5). Five-type: 84.94 / 87.43 / −1.94 / 98.16 vs SMoLoRA 83.02 / 85.05 / −6.50 / 98.12, DirLoRA 88.09 / 91.49 / 0.00 / 98.31 (Table 1). Parameters: total expansion 985.1 MB vs 2,143.9 MB for DirLoRA (−54 %), per task 104.6 vs 357.3 MB (−70 %) (Table 2, p. 6). Ablation: sharing A alone AP 85.38; adding B decomposition without L_reg drops to 73.99, BWT −16.14; with L_reg 84.88 (Table 2). Qwen2-VL-2B: LiLoRA AP 64.63 vs DirLoRA 67.55 (Table 5; values read from the text extraction).
- Limitations stated by the authors: none stated (no limitations section). In the results LiLoRA stays below the DirLoRA upper reference on AP and MAP in both settings (Table 1).

## Relevance (NOT from the paper)
Its two references, DirLoRA and SeqLoRA, correspond to this thesis's task-specific LoRA and shared LoRA; LiLoRA sits between them and shows the gap the shared/task-specific split closes (AP 46.21 → 84.88 vs 88.13), though in a 7B MLLM with full-size instruction datasets, not few-shot 3D segmentation.
