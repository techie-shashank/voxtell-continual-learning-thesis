## Uncited or unsupported claims in the range

1. **l. 259–260 (must fix).** "In most of the systems in this and the previous subsection, what is added for a task is held in components of its own, reached through the task identity, a learned selector or a class-specific head. MedSeqFT and RegCL are the exceptions that change shared parameters." Not supported by the works reviewed. In Sec. 2.4.1 most cited systems change shared parameters: Baweja (EWC), Ozdemir (distillation + exemplars), ILT, MiB, Liu et al. 2022/2023, Bera et al. (replay), SegGAN, C²GR (one shared segmentor), Lifelong nnU-Net (rehearsal, EWC, LwF, RW, MiB on one nnU-Net), Ranem et al., Beyond Forgetting (regularisation and replay families). Only González et al., Continual Segment, CL-Net, Low-Rank MoE and, in part, Zhang/Liu (shared backbone plus class heads) hold additions in components of their own. In Sec. 2.4.2, CLoPA also changes shared parameters (normalisation and shallow convolution weights of the one model), so the exceptions are at least MedSeqFT, RegCL and CLoPA. MedSeqFT also adds a new head per task. Hint: limit the statement to the parameter-isolation paragraph and the foundation-model methods, and list CLoPA, or drop "most".
2. **l. 190 (should fix).** "Continual segmentation adds a problem that classification does not have: the masks of one task may label as background a structure that another task labels as foreground." There is no citation. Cite the background-shift formulation of MiB (`cermelli2020modelingbackgroundincrementallearning`, Sec. 1/3), which is already in the bib. The colon reveal also breaks the style rules.
3. **l. 192 (should fix).** "Since a CT or MRI volume shows the whole anatomical region, every scan in such a sequence contains structures that its masks leave unlabelled." This is the thesis's own reasoning, and liu2022 does not state it (tier-A review). "Every scan" is stronger than the evidence. Hint: "typically contains", marked as own reasoning, or cite the partial-label statement of Liu et al. ("there exist many partially labeled datasets", Sec. 1) or Ji et al. (label conflict, Sec. 1, p. 2).
4. **l. 193 (ok).** "VoxTell predicts one binary mask per text prompt" is backed by the cross-reference: background.tex l. 74 cites VoxTell for "a segmentation mask of the described structure", and the binary cross-entropy loss is in paper_notes/voxtell.md, Sec. 3. "The masks of each task label one structure" follows from the task definition in CLAUDE.md. The conclusion that "the conflict does not reach the output" is the thesis's own reasoning and needs no citation. Nothing to fix.
5. **l. 217 (should fix).** "Parameter-isolation methods give each task or structure components that later tasks do not change." This is contradicted by l. 221 in the same paragraph: CL-Net "updates existing decoders by a moving average when an anatomy reappears" (Guo et al., Sec. 2, p. 8). Hint: "that later tasks do not change, or change only when the same structure reappears".
6. **l. 243 (should fix).** "Several methods add knowledge to SAM-based models, which are prompted with points or boxes." CLoPA (l. 249) adapts nnInteractive, a 3D CNN U-Net, which is not SAM-based (CLoPA card, Sec. 2.1). Either reword the topic sentence ("spatially promptable models") or move CLoPA.
7. **l. 252 (minor).** "Two works adapt 3D foundation models without spatial prompts." MedSeqFT's VoCo and UniMiSS+ are self-supervised CT encoders with a new randomly initialised head per task (Sec. III-D, IV-B), not segmentation foundation models, while the subsection is titled "Pretrained Segmentation Foundation Models". Make the distinction explicit, e.g. "a self-supervised 3D encoder".
8. **l. 223 vs l. 228 (should fix).** The two lines give inconsistent values for the same BTCV→LiTS result of Zhang et al.: 78.5 % as reported by Chen et al., and 0.817 as reported by Zhang et al. See the table.
