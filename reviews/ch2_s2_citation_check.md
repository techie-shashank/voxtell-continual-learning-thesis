## Specific checks requested
1. **Numbers** (metric/setting): all FSEFT, SEA-PEFT, UniverSeg and CLIP-LoRA values match their tables. Exceptions: the UniverSeg training-data description (l. 75) and the FSEFT LoRA novel-structure average of 38.49, which is internally inconsistent in the paper (l. 81). The metric and setting are named in every sentence except l. 75–76 for UniverSeg, which should say "held-out datasets, 2D".
2. **Locators**: Sec. 2.2 is correct (2.2.1 would be more exact). Sec. 3.3.4 exists but is titled "Other", so the label "transfer learning" is the thesis's paraphrase of the Table 5 category. Sec. 5.6 is correct (5.6 "Generalization", item "Rapid Adaptation").
3. **"the review groups [this meaning] under transfer learning"**: not a fair reading as worded. The review has a single few-shot definition (novel classes, usually episodic). Sec. 3.3.4 collects FSS *models* that use transfer learning without a meta-learning algorithm, still evaluated in the novel-class sense. It does not group a second *meaning* of "few-shot" anywhere.
4. **"Neither paper evaluates the adapted model on anything but the target"** (l. 84, uncited): consistent with both sources. FSEFT mentions catastrophic forgetting only as motivation (Sec. 1, 3.2.2) and measures only the target organs. SEA-PEFT reports only target Dice on its hold-out test set. It needs `\cite{silvarodriguez2023fseft,ly2026seapeft}`. FSEFT's FLARE'22 experiment evaluates the nine organs jointly, which is still the target.
5. **"choosing the adapted matrices and the rank is difficult"**: supported (Sec. 7, "not trivial"). The attached claim that the configuration was "chosen from ablations on three of the datasets" is not.
6. **Close paraphrase**: no sentence is near-verbatim. Closest is l. 90 ("choosing the adapted matrices and the rank is difficult" vs "selecting the matrices to adapt and determining the corresponding rank … is not trivial"). It is short and explicitly attributed, so it is acceptable. L. 73 and l. 94 paraphrase with clearly different wording.

## Uncited non-obvious claims in the range
- l. 72 "The term few-shot has two meanings in medical image segmentation." This is the thesis's own distinction. The cited review gives only one definition. Either support it with a source that uses few-shot for fine-tuning (FSEFT does, l. 79), or phrase it as the thesis's distinction ("is used in two senses").
- l. 84 "Neither paper evaluates the adapted model on anything but the target …". The claim is correct but has no `\cite`. Add both keys.
- l. 85 "Their shot counts of 1 to 10 labelled volumes …". Correct for the few-shot regime. Note that FSEFT also reports K = 30.
- l. 93 "All of these works adapt a model to one task, each time starting from the same pretrained weights." UniverSeg updates no weights (l. 74), so "adapt … starting from pretrained weights" does not fit it, and the review is not an adapting work. Restrict to FSEFT, SEA-PEFT and CLIP-LoRA.
- l. 95 "only Chen et al. adapt a 3D model to a sequence of tasks with few labelled examples each". This is a search-scope claim with no `\cite`. Add `\cite{chen2026fewshotcontinuallearning3d}` (key exists as `papers/text/chen2026fewshotcontinuallearning3d.txt`; check it in the bib). The claim is checkable only against the search protocol in `\cref{sec:sota-summary}`.
