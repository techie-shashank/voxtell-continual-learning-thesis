\chapter{State of the Art}
\label{chap:sota}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
This thesis adapts a pretrained free-text promptable 3D segmentation model to a sequence of new target structures, each with few labelled volumes.
The related work therefore comes from four areas: text-promptable medical image segmentation, few-shot adaptation of segmentation models, continual learning for pretrained vision--language models, and continual learning for medical image segmentation.
The chapter reviews them in this order and closes with a summary of all reviewed works and the gap that this thesis addresses.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Text-Promptable Medical Image Segmentation}
\label{sec:text-promptable-seg}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Promptable segmentation models differ in how the target is selected: either by a spatial prompt marked in the image or by a text prompt that names the target.
MedSAM and SAM-Med2D, two 2D adaptations of the \gls{sam}~\cite{Kirillov_2023_ICCV} to medical images, take spatial prompts.
MedSAM is prompted with bounding boxes~\cite{Ma_2024}, and SAM-Med2D with boxes, points and masks~\cite{cheng2023sammed2d}.
SegVol, a 3D model for \gls{ct}, accepts both kinds of prompt~\cite{du2025segvoluniversalinteractivevolumetric}.
Besides points and boxes, it takes category names, which it inserts into a fixed template and embeds with a frozen CLIP text encoder~\cite{radford2021learningtransferablevisualmodels}.
It was first pretrained on about 96{,}000 \gls{ct} volumes without using labels and then fine-tuned on about 6{,}000 labelled volumes covering more than 200 categories.
Other work keeps the target class fixed and uses text as additional input when labels are scarce, as LViT does with radiologist-verified descriptions of lung infections, which it needs at inference as well~\cite{li2023lvitlanguagemeetsvision}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Several text-promptable models select their target from a fixed vocabulary.
The CLIP-Driven Universal Model feeds the CLIP embedding of a template such as ``A computerized tomography of a [CLS]'' to a controller that generates the output convolutions of each class~\cite{Liu_2023}.
It was trained on 3{,}410 \gls{ct} scans from 14 public datasets with 25 organs and 6 tumour types.
SAT extends text prompting to \gls{ct}, \gls{mri} and \gls{pet} volumes: a text encoder trained on medical knowledge embeds terms from a vocabulary of 497 classes, and a query decoder matches them with the features of a 3D U-Net~\cite{zhao2025sat}.
In the authors' demonstrations, free-form input such as a radiology report is first converted into terms of this vocabulary by a separate language model.
On 72 datasets with 22{,}186 scans, SAT's larger variant, SAT-Pro, reached an average \gls{dsc} of 78.81\,\% over all classes, which the authors report as comparable to the 80.54\,\% of 72 dataset-specific nnU-Nets.
BiomedParse~\cite{zhao2025foundation-9de} and MedSAM-3~\cite{liu2026medsam3delvingsegmentmedical} also take text prompts, but they segment 2D images.
On 3D data, SAT-Pro was better than BiomedParse on 29 of 30 categories, and the SAT authors attribute BiomedParse's false positives to its slice-wise design~\cite{zhao2025sat}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
VoxTell, the model adapted in this thesis, accepts a text prompt that may be a single anatomical term or a full clinical sentence~\cite{rokuss2025voxtellfreetextpromptableuniversal}.
It was trained on more than 62{,}000 \gls{ct}, \gls{mri} and \gls{pet} volumes from 158 public datasets with 1{,}087 anatomical and pathological concepts.
Unlike SAT, it fuses the text with the image features at every decoder resolution.
On eleven datasets that were not used for training, its mean zero-shot \gls{dsc} was 70.85\,\%, against 51.23\,\% for SAT, but on the knee \gls{mri} of SKM-TEA~\cite{desai2021skmtea} it reached only 10.72\,\%~\cite[Suppl.~Table~9]{rokuss2025voxtellfreetextpromptableuniversal}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
The VoxTell authors report robustness to synonyms, rephrasings and typos~\cite{rokuss2025voxtellfreetextpromptableuniversal}.
SEER measured how five text-promptable models, four of them 3D, respond to free-text requests that a language model generated in eight clinical styles~\cite{zhang2026seer}.
Among these models, VoxTell kept the highest \gls{dsc} under free-text requests, on average and in the worst case, whereas the \gls{dsc} of SAT on the pelvic-bone dataset PENGWIN fell from 96.05\,\% with the class-name prompt to 0.01\,\%.
Even for VoxTell, the worst rephrasing lowered the \gls{dsc} on PENGWIN from 97.59\,\% with the class-name prompt to 79.34\,\%.

\section{Few-Shot Adaptation of Segmentation Models}
\label{sec:sota-few-shot}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Each task in this thesis comes with only a few labelled training volumes, so this section reviews how pretrained models have been adapted to a single target with few labels.
In the review by Dissanayake et al., a few-shot model has to segment a new class from one or a few labelled examples, and it can do so in two ways~\cite[Sec.~2.2]{theekshana26fewshot}.
In the first, the model receives the labelled examples as input together with the image to segment, so a new class often needs no change to its weights.
UniverSeg is an example of this approach for 2D medical images~\cite{universeg}.
In the second, the weights of a trained model are updated on the labelled examples, for instance by fine-tuning~\cite[Sec.~2.2]{theekshana26fewshot}.
This thesis follows the second approach: a pretrained model is fine-tuned on a few labelled volumes of each task, and one labelled training volume counts as one shot.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Silva-Rodríguez et al.\ fine-tuned a Swin UNETR, pretrained on partially labelled \gls{ct} scans, to one organ of TotalSegmentator at a time with 1, 5 or 10 labelled volumes~\cite{silvarodriguez2023fseft}.
This range overlaps with the 5, 10 and 20 labelled volumes per task used in this thesis.
On nine organs that were already among the pretraining classes, \gls{lora}~\cite{hu2022lora} in the encoder and full fine-tuning differed by less than one percentage point of mean \gls{dsc} at 5 and 10 shots, and only LoRA improved from 5 to 10 shots~\cite{silvarodriguez2023fseft}.
On 16 structures that were new to the model, parameter-efficient methods in the encoder alone reached at most 40.46\,\% at 10 shots, below full fine-tuning (52.38\,\%) and fine-tuning the decoder together with LoRA in the encoder (57.39\,\%).
SEA-PEFT uses the same pretrained model and organs and selects the type, position and rank of the adapters automatically during training~\cite{ly2026seapeft}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
For vision--language models, CLIP-LoRA adds rank-2 LoRA modules to the query, key and value projections of every layer of both CLIP encoders~\cite{zanella2024lowrankfewshotadaptationvisionlanguage}.
It uses one set of hyperparameters for eleven 2D image classification datasets with 1 to 16 shots per class.
With a ViT-B/16 image encoder, its average top-1 accuracy was the best of the compared methods at every number of shots, for example 72.5\,\% against 70.9\,\% at one shot.
In the authors' ablation, the value or output projection was the best single matrix to adapt, and adapting more matrices or using a higher rank could lower the accuracy.
The authors note that choosing the adapted matrices and the rank is not trivial, and we examine the rank in our setting in \cref{sec:exp-rank}.
Like CLIP-LoRA, we apply LoRA to attention projections, but only to the query and value projections of the cross-attention in the prompt decoder of VoxTell (\cref{subsec:method-lora}).
CoOp and CoCoOp adapt a frozen CLIP in another way, by training context vectors in front of the class name~\cite{zhou2022coop,zhou2022cocoop}.
The meta-net of our prompt-tuning strategy is inspired by CoCoOp.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
The works above adapt a model to one target at a time, each time starting from the same pretrained weights.
The two 3D studies evaluate the adapted model only on that target, so they do not show how much of its earlier ability the model loses through the adaptation~\cite{silvarodriguez2023fseft,ly2026seapeft}.
In this thesis, the same model is trained on the tasks one after another, so each adaptation can also affect the tasks trained before it.
Among the works found in our search, only Chen et al.\ adapt a pretrained 3D medical model to a sequence of tasks with few labelled examples each, one of them a segmentation task~\cite{chen2026fewshotcontinuallearning3d}.

\section[Continual Learning for Pretrained Vision--Language Models]{Continual Learning for Pretrained\texorpdfstring{\newline}{ } Vision--Language Models}
\label{sec:cl-vlm}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
VoxTell combines a frozen text encoder with an image encoder and lets the encoded text prompt attend to the image features, which makes it a vision--language model.
Methods that add tasks to such a model while keeping its text-conditioned abilities have been developed for pretrained vision--language models such as CLIP~\cite{radford2021learningtransferablevisualmodels}, and this section reviews them.
They were developed for 2D images and evaluated on classification or on tasks with text output.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Continual-learning scenarios differ in whether the task identity of an input is given at inference~\cite{van_de_Ven_2025}.
Independently of the scenario, De Lange et al.\ divide continual-learning methods into three families according to how they preserve information about earlier tasks~\cite[Sec.~3]{delange2022}.
Replay methods reuse stored or generated samples of earlier tasks while learning a new one.
Regularisation methods add a loss term, either distillation from an earlier model or a penalty on changes to important parameters.
Parameter-isolation methods assign different parameters to different tasks, which typically requires the task identity at prediction time.
For vision--language models, Liu et al.\ group methods into multimodal replay, cross-modal regularisation and parameter-efficient adaptation, and note that many methods combine these families~\cite[Sec.~4]{liu2026continuallearningvlmssurvey}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
The multimodal replay method reviewed here avoids storing real images by generating the replayed data.
GIFT fine-tunes all parameters of CLIP on each task together with 1{,}000 images that Stable Diffusion generates from class names of earlier tasks and of ImageNet~\cite{wu2024gift}.
On these images, it distils the image--text similarities of the model from before the task.
Replay of stored real samples, which our replay strategy uses, is reviewed for medical image segmentation in \cref{subsec:cl-medseg-net}, where Bera et al.\ also compare how the stored data are selected.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
ZSCL, a cross-modal regularisation method, showed that continual fine-tuning of CLIP also lowers its zero-shot accuracy on datasets that it has not been trained on yet~\cite{zheng2023preventingzeroshottransferdegradation}.
Its benchmark trains eleven classification datasets as tasks in two task orders, Order~I and Order~II, and reports Transfer, the mean accuracy on each task before the task is trained, and Last accuracy, the mean accuracy on all tasks after the last one.
To keep the zero-shot accuracy, ZSCL fine-tunes all parameters and distils the image--text similarity distributions of the frozen initial model on a reference dataset, by default 100{,}000 ImageNet images, and additionally constrains the weights during training.
On Order~I, this raised Transfer from 44.6\,\% for sequential fine-tuning to 68.1\,\%, close to the 69.4\,\% of the zero-shot model, and Last accuracy from 77.3\,\% to 83.6\,\%.
In the authors' ablation, distilling on the images of the current task instead of a reference dataset kept Transfer at the same level (56.7\,\% compared with 56.8\,\%) but lowered Last accuracy from 83.0\,\% to 80.2\,\%~\cite[Table~1b]{zheng2023preventingzeroshottransferdegradation}.
The authors name the need for a reference dataset as the limitation of ZSCL.
The distillation-based strategy of this thesis is adapted from ZSCL (\cref{subsec:method-distillation}).
SPU measures the loss of zero-shot accuracy outside the task sequence, on a control set, the ImageNet validation set, by comparing the adapted model with the frozen pretrained model~\cite{Zhang_2024_CVPR}.
Our control task follows this idea.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
PRIMED transfers the distillation on reference data of ZSCL to medical 2D image classification with BiomedCLIP~\cite{chen2025forgingdynamicmemoryretrievalguided}.
It was evaluated on two task sequences that include X-ray, pathology, fundus, skin, cell and endoscopy images, with the task identity given at inference.
Instead of a fixed reference dataset, it retrieves image--caption pairs related to the tasks from 18~million PubMed figures and distils on them.
Unlike ZSCL, PRIMED distils from the model of the previous task, and in its ablation this teacher gave a higher Last accuracy than the initial model (82.1\,\% compared with 78.2\,\%)~\cite[Table~4a]{chen2025forgingdynamicmemoryretrievalguided}.
The ablation of ZSCL had found the opposite, a Last accuracy of 83.0\,\% with the initial model and 80.7\,\% with the previous one~\cite[Table~1d]{zheng2023preventingzeroshottransferdegradation}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Parameter-efficient methods freeze most of the pretrained model and train a small set of added parameters~\cite[Sec.~4.3]{liu2026continuallearningvlmssurvey}.
Liu et al.\ note that tasks sharing the low-rank space of one LoRA can still interfere.
Some methods therefore keep the updates of new tasks orthogonal to the subspaces of earlier tasks.
LiLoRA, for continual instruction tuning of the multimodal language model LLaVA, shares one LoRA factor across tasks and splits the other into a shared and a task-specific part~\cite{che2026loraloraparameterefficientarchitecture}.
In its Single-type setting, one LoRA trained sequentially on all tasks reached an average accuracy of only 46.21\,\%, against 88.13\,\% for a separate LoRA per task.
LiLoRA came close to the separate LoRAs with less than half their added parameters.
MSLoRA-CR adds a LoRA branch per task to the frozen medical model LLaVA-Med and sums all branches learned so far~\cite{Zhang_2025}.
It was evaluated on 2D question answering, classification and report generation, with the task identity given at inference, and scored higher over all task metrics than a separate LoRA per task.
MoE-Adapters adds a mixture of LoRA experts with one router per task to a frozen CLIP~\cite{yu2024boostingcontinuallearningvisionlanguage}.
At inference, one autoencoder per task selects the router, and the authors observed that this selection assigned samples of one task to none of the tasks.
A separate LoRA per task and one LoRA shared by all tasks are the two LoRA strategies compared in this thesis.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Other methods train learned prompt tokens, which are vectors added to the input of a frozen model.
S-Prompts learns separate prompt tokens for each domain and selects them at inference through the nearest of the image-feature centroids stored per domain~\cite{wang2023spromptslearningpretrainedtransformers}.
CoLeCLIP learns one prompt token per task in the frozen image encoder and keeps a single vocabulary of class text embeddings for all tasks~\cite{li2024coleclipopendomaincontinuallearning}.
CPE-CLIP targets few-shot class-incremental learning with five examples per new class~\cite{D_Alessandro_2023}.
It learns prompt tokens in both CLIP encoders, couples them through a learned projection and scales their gradients down as more classes have been seen, training about 400{,}000 parameters.
In its ablation on CUB200, a fine-grained bird classification dataset, removing the gradient scaling lowered the accuracy more than removing any other component~\cite[Sec.~5.5, Fig.~2]{D_Alessandro_2023}.
The prompt-tuning strategy of this thesis is adapted from CPE-CLIP (\cref{subsec:method-prompt}).

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
These methods do not carry over directly to VoxTell.
ZSCL, GIFT and PRIMED distil distributions over the similarities between an image and a set of texts~\cite{zheng2023preventingzeroshottransferdegradation,wu2024gift,chen2025forgingdynamicmemoryretrievalguided}.
VoxTell predicts one mask per text prompt and does not compute such distributions.
CPE-CLIP places prompt tokens in transformer encoders~\cite{D_Alessandro_2023}, whereas the image encoder of VoxTell is convolutional.
\Cref{sec:method-strategies} describes how our strategies adapt these methods.

\section[Continual Learning for Medical Image Segmentation]{Continual Learning for Medical Image\texorpdfstring{\newline}{ } Segmentation}
\label{sec:cl-medseg}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Unlike the methods for vision--language models, which classify images or generate text, the methods reviewed in this section segment medical images.
In continual segmentation, a structure that is foreground in the masks of one task can be labelled as background in the masks of another, which is known as background shift~\cite{cermelli2020modelingbackgroundincrementallearning}.
In the multi-organ \gls{ct} sequences of Liu et al., for example, the liver is foreground in the first dataset and background in the later ones~\cite{liu2022learningincrementallysegmentmultiple}.
A foundation model is pretrained on large and diverse data and then adapted to many downstream tasks\todo{cite: definition of foundation model}.
\Cref{subsec:cl-medseg-net} reviews networks that do not build on a foundation model, and \cref{subsec:cl-medseg-fm,subsec:cl-medseg-text} review foundation models without and with text prompts.

\subsection{Networks Without a Foundation Model}
\label{subsec:cl-medseg-net}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Early work on continual learning for medical image segmentation applied regularisation and distillation.
Baweja et al.\ used \gls{ewc}~\cite{Kirkpatrick_2017} for brain \gls{mri}~\cite{baweja2018continuallearningmedicalimaging}, and Ozdemir et al.\ combined distillation from the previous model with a small set of stored examples for shoulder \gls{mri}~\cite{Ozdemir_2018}.
ILT, a method from natural-image segmentation, distils both the output and the encoder features of the previous model~\cite{michieli2019incrementallearningtechniquessemantic}.
Several of the works reviewed below use it as a baseline~\cite{ji2023continualsegmentsingleunified,liu2024universalextensible}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
In natural-image segmentation, MiB was the first method to explicitly address background shift, by treating old classes as possibly present in the background of a new task~\cite{cermelli2020modelingbackgroundincrementallearning}.
Like ILT, it is a baseline in several of the medical works reviewed below~\cite{ji2023continualsegmentsingleunified,gonzalez2023lifelong}.
In medical image segmentation, P.~Liu et al.\ used a marginal loss for partially labelled multi-organ \gls{ct}~\cite{liu2022learningincrementallysegmentmultiple}, and X.~Liu et al.\ used complementary pseudo-labels for structures added one after another in 2D brain-tumour \gls{mri}~\cite{liu2023incrementallearningheterogeneousstructure}.
Our masks also label the structures of the other tasks as background, but each mask is the target for one text prompt only, so no voxel receives different labels for the same text prompt.
We therefore do not adopt these methods.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Replay methods for segmentation either store selected parts of earlier data or generate the replayed data.
Bera et al.\ keep crops around the target instead of full volumes and select them by the fraction of target voxels and by training difficulty~\cite{memory_replay_cropping}.
On a sequence of prostate, spleen and hippocampus segmentation, twelve such crops gave an average \gls{dsc} of $83.3 \pm 1.3$\,\%, against $78.3 \pm 1.3$\,\% for three randomly chosen full volumes.
C$^2$GR trains a universal segmentation network from scratch on 20 tasks from eight modalities, all as 2D images or slices, and replays image--mask pairs generated by a diffusion model~\cite{li2026c2gr}.
Each task is specified by a fixed text template that names the modality, the region and the target.
After the last task, the mean \gls{dsc} over all tasks was 83.62\,\%, against 86.06\,\% for joint training and 25.23\,\% for sequential fine-tuning.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Parameter-isolation methods keep separate parts of the model for different tasks or structures.
Continual Segment freezes an encoder trained on TotalSegmentator and adds one pruned decoder per later dataset~\cite{ji2023continualsegmentsingleunified}, and its extension CL-Net adds decoders for new anatomies~\cite{guo2025continuallearningdrivenmodelaccurate}.
In an extension of the CLIP-Driven Universal Model, Zhang et al.\ share the encoder and decoder across all classes, keep only the output heads class-specific and train the earlier classes on pseudo-labels from the previous model~\cite{zhang2023continuallearningabdominalmultiorgan,liu2024universalextensible}.
The weights of each head are generated by an \gls{mlp} of its own from the pooled image features and the CLIP embedding of the class template.
After 13 BTCV organs followed by the LiTS liver tumour, the mean \gls{dsc} was 0.817 on the organs and 0.466 on the tumour, against 0.770 and 0.456 with pseudo-labels alone.
Since each class has to be named in advance and adds a head, the model grows with every class.
In our setting, the text prompt names the target structure, and all strategies except task-specific \gls{lora} use one parameter set for all tasks.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Some studies compare existing methods instead of proposing a new one.
Lifelong nnU-Net trains an nnU-Net through sequences of hippocampus, prostate and cardiac \gls{mri} datasets~\cite{gonzalez2023lifelong}.
Replay, which the authors call rehearsal, showed the least forgetting, whereas \gls{ewc} and MiB preserved the performance on earlier tasks at the expense of the new ones.
On the last hippocampus dataset, for example, \gls{ewc} and MiB reached a \gls{dsc} of 31.93\,\% and 20.75\,\%, compared with 90.92\,\% for sequential training.
The authors also found the results to depend on the task order and recommend comparing methods over several orders, and Ranem et al.\ found the difference in forgetting between nnU-Net and a vision-transformer variant to depend on the method used~\cite{ranem2022continualhippocampussegmentationtransformers}.
The Beyond Forgetting benchmark compares 15 continual-learning methods on 2D slices for new domains, new structures in the same images and new organs in new images, and reports plasticity and forward transfer besides forgetting~\cite{wang2026beyondforgetting}.
It names no single best method, but replay gave the best trade-off between retaining earlier tasks and learning new ones, and parameter isolation avoided forgetting at the cost of a growing model size.
For the new-domain sequences, the task order affected the results.

\subsection{Foundation Models Without Text Prompts}
\label{subsec:cl-medseg-fm}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
SAM-Med3D-MoE and CA-SAM add task-specific parts to a pretrained \gls{sam}-based model, which is prompted with points or boxes, and keep the pretrained model unchanged~\cite{wang2024sammed3dmoe,wang2025casam}.
SAM-Med3D-MoE fine-tunes one copy of the mask decoder of SAM-Med3D for each downstream task or poorly segmented class, separately rather than in a sequence.
A gate selects one of these experts or falls back to the original decoder.
With box prompts, the mean \gls{dsc} on 15 poorly segmented classes rose from 0.532 to 0.564, whereas on the classes that no expert was trained for it fell from 0.541 to 0.400~\cite[Table~1]{wang2024sammed3dmoe}.
CA-SAM inserts one small alignment network per dataset between the frozen image encoder and the mask decoder of SAM, and one variational autoencoder per task selects the network or passes unfamiliar inputs to the frozen SAM.
On nine 2D datasets learned in sequence, its final mean \gls{iou} was 76.12\,\%, against 78.43\,\% for joint training and 53.81\,\% for zero-shot SAM.
On five datasets outside the sequence, its \gls{iou} after the last task stayed within 5.2 percentage points of the frozen SAM, whereas Learning without Forgetting~\cite{li2017learningforgetting} on the same alignment network lost 16.6 to 46.5 percentage points~\cite[Table~2]{wang2025casam}.
Both evaluations measure how much pretrained ability is kept outside the adapted tasks, as our control task does.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Two works adapt pretrained 3D models without spatial prompts.
MedSeqFT fine-tunes a self-supervised \gls{ct} model through five segmentation tasks in sequence, with a new head per task~\cite{ye2025medseqft}.
It adds distillation on stored samples, which are chosen by their pretraining loss, and a LoRA-based refinement that is merged into the weights.
Its mean \gls{dsc} over the five tasks was 68.3\,\%, against 65.3\,\% for fine-tuning a separate copy of the pretrained model per task.
The paper reports no results on earlier tasks after later ones and no measure of forgetting.
Chen et al.\ freeze a 3D brain-\gls{mri} foundation model and train one LoRA module and one head per task, selected by the task identity at inference~\cite{chen2026fewshotcontinuallearning3d}.
The two tasks, tumour segmentation and brain-age regression, each have 16 or 32 labelled samples.
With 32 samples, the tumour \gls{dsc} of this design was $0.60 \pm 0.08$ and unaffected by the second task, whereas sequential fine-tuning reached $0.80 \pm 0.02$ and fell to $0.16 \pm 0.19$ after the second task.

\subsection{Text-Promptable Foundation Models}
\label{subsec:cl-medseg-text}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Three recent works by Gao and colleagues are the closest to this thesis: they train a text-promptable segmentation model on a sequence of medical segmentation tasks~\cite{gao2026medcrpcl,gao2025paewc,gao2026medpeftcl}.
All three use the 2D model CLIPSeg, treat each dataset or dataset subset as a task, mix endoscopy, dermoscopy, ultrasound and X-ray images, and train on the full training sets.
MedCRP-CL and MedPEFT-CL take their text prompts from earlier work on medical vision--language segmentation, which includes descriptive sentences per image~\cite{gao2026medcrpcl,gao2026medpeftcl}.
PA-EWC uses templated phrases that range from class names to combinations of visual, spatial and medical descriptions~\cite{gao2025paewc}.

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
MedCRP-CL clusters the tasks by the CLIP embeddings of their text prompts and trains one LoRA module per cluster, with EWC within each cluster~\cite{gao2026medcrpcl}.
On 16 tasks, its mean \gls{dsc} after the sequence was 73.3\,\% with 4.1\,\% forgetting, compared with 65.3\,\% and 7.1\,\% for MoE-Adapters and 48.0\,\% and 28.3\,\% for sequential fine-tuning.
PA-EWC weights the EWC penalty of different parameter groups by the complexity of the text prompts~\cite{gao2025paewc}.
On five tasks, it reports a higher mean \gls{dsc} and less forgetting than ZSCL and MoE-Adapters, although the averages in its summary table do not match its per-task results~\cite[Tables~3 and~5]{gao2025paewc}.
MedPEFT-CL adds LoRA to both CLIPSeg encoders, reuses the adapter of an earlier task when the text embeddings of the two tasks are similar, and replays the hardest 15\,\% of each task's training samples~\cite{gao2026medpeftcl}.
On a sequence of ten tasks, its mean \gls{dsc} was 78.19\,\%, against 74.15\,\% for MoE-Adapters and 81.02\,\% for one model per task.
For MedCRP-CL and MedPEFT-CL, the papers do not state which adapter is used at test time, so it is unclear whether the adapter is selected by the task identity or by the text prompt~\cite{gao2026medcrpcl,gao2026medpeftcl}.

\section{Summary and Research Gap}
\label{sec:sota-summary}

% CLAUDE-DRAFT
Text-promptable 3D models such as SAT and VoxTell are trained once on many structures, and VoxTell's zero-shot results on the knee \gls{mri} of SKM-TEA show that this generalisation does not extend to the knee structures.
Few-shot fine-tuning adapts 3D segmentation models to one new target with few labelled volumes and evaluates only that target.
Continual-learning methods for pretrained vision--language models protect the pretrained representation while tasks are added and were evaluated on classification or on tasks with text output rather than on segmentation.
Among the continual-learning works for medical image segmentation, only Chen et al.\ adapt with few labelled samples, and those conditioned on text are either 2D or limited to a fixed set of class names.

The closest works each lack part of this setting: MedCRP-CL, PA-EWC and MedPEFT-CL adapt the 2D model CLIPSeg with full training sets, and Chen et al.\ adapt a 3D model with few samples but without text prompts and with the task identity given at inference.
None of these four works measures how much of the pretrained model's ability outside the task sequence is kept at the end of the sequence.
SPU measures such a loss for CLIP, and CA-SAM and SAM-Med3D-MoE for models prompted with points or boxes.
